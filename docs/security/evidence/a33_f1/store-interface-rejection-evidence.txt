2. Correction and ownership boundary

Only executable change:
  crates/qbind-consensus/tests/a33_f1_quarantine_store_interface_tests.rs
SHA-256: 23fa1680b367a03bd02d09f1c5a2b16b62c2809bc1550a3e82c9b6eb2ad30041

record_outcome now recognizes the active operation before fallible checks:
the supplied scope must equal the token scope, store/incarnation must match
this fake store and the stored acknowledged intent must match that exact
scope/intent revision. Recognition closes outcome_attempted and keeps
unresolved true BEFORE checking current writer ownership, expected revision,
revision overflow, version, input bounds or aggregate merge bounds.
Therefore legitimate active-operation errors cannot leave or restore the
fixture mutation gate, even if ownership/revision inputs are later repaired
or a valid recording retry succeeds. No latch-clearing operation is added.

RecordFailure explicitly distinguishes:
  RejectedUnrecorded: no submitted update was merged or published; observed
    is None. The unchanged borrowed Update remains with its caller/owner,
    including all rejected bytes, prefix and known effects.
  PublicationUnresolved: valid evidence merged into volatile fake state;
    publication failed, was uncertain or was visible before error. An
    observed candidate is returned, NOT an acknowledged durability claim.
Previous visible records, known prefix/effects and revision remain unchanged
on pre-merge rejection; only the recognized active operation's latch changes.
No oversized or unsupported input is truncated, downgraded or silently
replaced. A later Unknown retry does not establish that the rejected
owner-held effects were absent or durably recorded. The caller must retain
the rejected update and failure alongside any later acknowledgement.

Unauthorized scope, foreign token, foreign store/incarnation/operation/writer
or wrong intent revision do not identify this active operation. They return
RejectedUnrecorded without changing its gate, evidence or revision. The
active acknowledged operation's owner is responsible for closing its own
operation when its recording fails; the store performs this closure when
that operation is recognized. An unrelated caller cannot latch or overwrite
someone else's acknowledged operation. A caller that supplies mismatched
credentials cannot infer that its own operation was latched elsewhere;
it must preserve its own evidence/error and withhold further effects under
its own owner/barrier. No production owner orchestration or authentication
mechanism is implemented by this fake interface.

Writer ownership loss with an otherwise recognized active token is different
from a foreign-writer token: it closes the active operation before returning
Ownership. Restoring the local fixture owner does not reopen the gate.
Stale expected revisions and revision overflow similarly close the active
operation. Scope errors intentionally precede ownership errors for unrelated
tokens; no remote reachability or production authorization is asserted.
